@ Precalculus > Domain of a Function > Stacked conditions | d5 | tags: functions, domain, radicals, logarithms
Q: Find the domain of $f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{x^2 - 9}}{\ln(x + 1)}$.
A: $[3, \infty)$
S: Three conditions here, not two. The root needs $x^2 - 9 \ge 0$, so $x \le -3$ or $x \ge 3$. The logarithm needs $x + 1 > 0$, so $x > -1$. And because the logarithm is a *denominator* it must also be non-zero: $\ln(x + 1) \ne 0$ means $x + 1 \ne 1$, so $x \ne 0$.

Now intersect. The branch $x \le -3$ cannot coexist with $x > -1$, so it disappears entirely. The branch $x \ge 3$ satisfies $x > -1$, and $x \ne 0$ is automatic there. What is left is $[3, \infty)$ — the third condition had to be checked even though it turned out not to exclude anything.
