Q: An arithmetic sequence starts ${{a}}$, ${{a + d}}$, ${{a + 2*d}}$, ${{a + 3*d}}$, … Find the common difference and the ${{n}}$th term.

Q: An arithmetic sequence has ${{p}}$th term ${{a + (p - 1)*d}}$ and ${{q}}$th term ${{a + (q - 1)*d}}$. Find its common difference and first term.
A: Common difference ${{d}}$; first term ${{a}}$
S: Going from the ${{p}}$th term to the ${{q}}$th takes ${{q - p}}$ steps, and the value changes by ${{a + (q - 1)*d}} - {{a + (p - 1)*d}} = {{(q - p)*d}}$. So each step is $\dfrac{ {{(q - p)*d}} }{ {{q - p}} } = {{d}}$. To get back to the first term, step back ${{p - 1}}$ times from the ${{p}}$th: ${{a + (p - 1)*d}} - ({{p - 1}})({{d}}) = {{a}}$. Counting steps rather than terms is the whole trick, and it is where the off-by-one usually creeps in.

@ Algebra 1 > Data > Line of best fit | d3 | tags: data, linear, applications
Q: A scatter plot of study hours $x$ against test score $y$ has line of best fit $y = {{coef(m,'x')}} {{signed(c)}}$. Predict the score for ${{h}}$ hours, and say what the slope means in context.
A: ${{m*h + c}}$. The slope means each extra hour of study is associated with about ${{m}}$ more marks.
S: Substituting $x = {{h}}$ gives $y = {{m}}({{h}}) {{signed(c)}} = {{m*h + c}}$. Interpreting the slope in context is the part worth practising: it is a rate, so its units are marks per hour, and it says that an extra hour goes with roughly ${{m}}$ extra marks. The intercept ${{c}}$ would be the predicted score after no study at all. Note the careful wording — "is associated with", not "causes": a line of best fit describes a pattern in data, and does not on its own establish that studying caused the change.

V: c = int 20..50
V: h = int 1..10
