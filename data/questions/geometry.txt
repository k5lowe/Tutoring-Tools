@ Geometry > Congruence > Criteria | d2 | tags: congruence, triangles
Q: Two triangles have two pairs of equal angles and the pair of sides between those angles equal. Which congruence criterion applies, and are the triangles congruent?
A: ASA. Yes, they are congruent.
S: Angle-Side-Angle: two angles and the included side determine a triangle completely. Once two angles are fixed the third follows from the angle sum, so the shape is fixed; the given side then fixes the size. Note that the side must be the one *between* the two angles for ASA — if it is not, the criterion is AAS, which also gives congruence but by a slightly different argument.

@ Geometry > Congruence > Two-column proof | d4 | tags: congruence, proof, triangles
Q: In quadrilateral $ABCD$, $AB = CD$ and $AB \parallel CD$. Prove that triangles $ABC$ and $CDA$ are congruent.
A: By SAS: $AB = CD$ (given), $\angle BAC = \angle DCA$ (alternate angles, $AB \parallel CD$), and $AC = CA$ (common side).
S: Draw the diagonal $AC$, which both triangles contain. The three matching parts are: $AB = CD$, which is given; $\angle BAC = \angle DCA$, alternate angles cut from the parallel lines $AB$ and $CD$ by the transversal $AC$; and $AC = CA$, the shared side, which is the step students most often forget to write down. The equal angle lies between the two equal sides in each triangle, so the criterion is SAS. It follows by CPCTC that $BC = DA$, which is how one proves that a quadrilateral with one pair of sides both equal and parallel must be a parallelogram.

@ Geometry > Quadrilaterals > Parallelogram properties | d3 | tags: quadrilaterals, proof
Q: The diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other. Prove that it is a parallelogram.
A: The diagonals split it into two pairs of congruent triangles by SAS, and CPCTC then gives equal alternate angles, so both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
S: Let the quadrilateral be $ABCD$ with diagonals meeting at $M$, where $AM = MC$ and $BM = MD$. Compare triangles $AMB$ and $CMD$: $AM = CM$ and $BM = DM$ are given, and $\angle AMB = \angle CMD$ because they are vertically opposite. That is SAS, so the triangles are congruent, and by CPCTC $\angle MAB = \angle MCD$. Those are alternate angles for the lines $AB$ and $CD$ cut by $AC$, so $AB \parallel CD$. @ Geometry > Quadrilaterals > Rhombus and rectangle | d3 | tags: quadrilaterals, diagonals, proof
Q: Explain why the diagonals of a rectangle are equal but the diagonals of a general parallelogram are not.
A: In a rectangle the two triangles formed by a diagonal and two sides are congruent by SAS with a right angle included, forcing equal diagonals; in a general parallelogram the included angles differ, so the diagonals differ.
S: Take parallelogram $ABCD$. Triangles $ABC$ and $BAD$ have $AB$ common, $BC = AD$ (opposite sides), and included angles $\angle ABC$ and $\angle BAD$. Here $s = \tfrac12({{9*k}} + {{10*k}} + {{17*k}}) = {{18*k}}$, so the area is $\sqrt{ {{18*k}} \times {{9*k}} \times {{8*k}} \times {{k}} } = \sqrt{ {{1296*k*k*k*k}} } = {{36*k*k}}$. It is worth checking the triangle exists before computing: ${{9*k}} + {{10*k}} = {{19*k}}$ is greater than ${{17*k}}$, so it does. If the sides fail the triangle inequality, one of the bracketed factors turns negative and the formula returns a square root of a negative number — which is the formula's way of reporting that there is no such triangle.
