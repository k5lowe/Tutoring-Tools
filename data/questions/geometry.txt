Q: Two sides of a triangle measure ${{a}}$ and ${{b}}$. Between what two values must the third side lie?
A: Greater than ${{abs(a - b)}}$ and less than ${{a + b}}$.
S: Any side must be shorter than the sum of the other two and longer than their difference: ${{a}} - {{b}}$ in absolute value is ${{abs(a - b)}}$, and ${{a}} + {{b}} = {{a + b}}$.

@ Geometry > Triangles > Exterior angle theorem | d2 | tags: triangles, angles
Q: In a triangle, two interior angles measure ${{a}}^\circ$ and ${{b}}^\circ$. Find the exterior angle at the third vertex.
A: ${{a + b}}^\circ$
S: An exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles: ${{a}} + {{b}} = {{a + b}}$ degrees. Equivalently, the third interior angle is ${{180 - a - b}}^\circ$, and $180 - {{180 - a - b}} = {{a + b}}$.
